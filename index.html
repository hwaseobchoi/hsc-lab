Supramolecular Ionic Solids

How can we design ionic crystals from molecular building blocks?

In inorganic ionic solids, coordination geometries such as tetrahedra and octahedra are often understood in terms of relative ionic sizes, as reflected in Pauling’s rules. For perovskites, the Goldschmidt tolerance factor provides a geometric guideline for structural stability. These approaches generally treat ions as spheres, but organic ions introduce additional complexity: they have diverse shapes and can participate in directional hydrogen bonding or exhibit chirality.

Our laboratory aims to uncover the design principles governing how non-spherical organic ions assemble through ionic interactions and hydrogen bonding. By connecting molecular shape and intermolecular interactions with crystal structure, we seek to create new families of supramolecular ionic solids with distinctive functional properties.

Ferroelectricity

Ferroelectric materials possess a spontaneous electric polarization that can be reversed by an applied electric field. This switchable polarization enables applications in non-volatile memory and neuromorphic computing, while related properties support pyroelectric energy harvesting, electrocaloric cooling, and nonlinear optics.

Ferroelectricity requires a polar crystal structure without inversion symmetry. Yet predicting and deliberately constructing such structures remains challenging. Our laboratory seeks to establish rational design strategies for geometric ferroelectricity by controlling the arrangement and collective distortions of molecular building blocks.

We explore how molecular chirality, hydrogen bonding, and chemical composition influence polar order and polarization switching. Through this approach, we aim to tune ferroelectric properties and discover new polarization-related phenomena in previously unexplored crystal structures.

Applications in Electronics and Energy

Solution-processable organic dielectric and ferroelectric materials offer opportunities for electronics and energy technologies. Their molecular tunability and compatibility with solution-based fabrication provide a versatile platform for developing functional devices.

We have fabricated field-effect transistors (FETs) and ferroelectric FETs using two-dimensional all-organic perovskites, exploring their potential for electronic and neuromorphic applications. Building on this work, we aim to extend these materials toward wafer-scale integration and scalable, solution-processed electronics.

We also seek to translate their polarization and thermal responses into energy applications, particularly pyroelectric energy harvesting and electrocaloric cooling. By connecting materials design with device engineering, our goal is to develop organic materials that combine useful functionality with scalable fabrication.
